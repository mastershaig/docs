---
title: "Fast Re-runs"
description: "How replay makes repeat runs faster and self-healing, and how to get the most from it"
---

The first time a test runs, Testavi works out each step live. On later runs it
**replays** the steps that already passed, which makes repeat runs much faster and
cheaper.

## How replay works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Passed steps are recorded">
    When a step passes, Testavi records the resolved action together with a robust
    way to find the element again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They are replayed deterministically">
    On the next run, a replayed step executes directly, skipping the planning the
    first run needed. A replayed step shows a **Replayed** badge in the timeline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Steps self-heal on a miss">
    If the page has changed and a replayed step no longer matches, Testavi falls back
    to working it out live again and re-records it — so a replay heals itself instead
    of failing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Changing a step's text invalidates its recording automatically, so edits always take
effect.

## Verify steps are always fresh

Replayed **action** steps are executed deterministically, but **Verify** steps are
always re-evaluated on every run. That means a replay still proves the important
things are true.

<Warning>
  A test made only of clicks, with no **Verify** step, re-checks nothing on a replay.
  **End every test with a Verify step** so a fast re-run still asserts something
  meaningful.
</Warning>

## Environment issues, not false failures

If a replay hits a blocking page — a **403, 429, Cloudflare bot-check, or captcha** —
Testavi reports it as an [environment issue](/run-results), not a product bug. A
transient block does not get logged as a regression in your store.

<Tip>
  Re-running only the failed tests or [scenario rows](/scenario-tables) is the fastest
  way to confirm a fix, because the passing steps around them replay.
</Tip>
